# JUXT AI Radar

### Introduction

This quarter the upheaval in software engineering reached another discipline: mathematics. In early October, OpenAI released hundreds of AI-generated proofs, many formalised in the Lean proof assistant. The community would have celebrated any mathematician who cracked even just one of these problems; yet here they arrived all together, solved apparently autonomously by one of OpenAI's unreleased models. Mathematicians did not celebrate. The mood was unease, bordering on anger. A proof has historically been a mechanism for the mathematical community to scaffold deeper understanding, a process this wave of machine-written solutions bypasses. Terence Tao wrote that open problems were now being "harvested at large scale in an unsustainable fashion, leaving entire fields of mathematics much less fertile".

For those outside of mathematics, the episode is a reminder of two things. Firstly, adoption: AI capability can arrive quickly and yet diffuse through working practices much more slowly. General-purpose technologies (such as electricity, computing and so on) tend to show significant lag between initial investment and productivity gains due to the time-consuming process of organisational adaptation. If AI is this kind of general-purpose technology then we appear to be close to the bottom of what economists would call the J-curve, where the heavy investment has yet to surface in productivity figures. Secondly, grounding: the results people trust are the ones Lean could check. A deterministic checker catches the model's errors. We see [behavioural specification languages](/ai-radar/languages-frameworks/#formal-specification-languages), our own [Allium](https://github.com/juxt/allium) among them, as the equivalent for software engineering. The closer a field comes to a formal representation of what 'correct' means, the more can be safely delegated.

This quarter the radar includes several other [techniques](/ai-radar/techniques/) for guiding and constraining AI output. [Harness engineering](/ai-radar/techniques/#harness-engineering), [context engineering](/ai-radar/techniques/#context-engineering), [evals as a release gate](/ai-radar/techniques/#evals-as-a-release-gate) and [orchestrator-worker agents](/ai-radar/techniques/#orchestrator-worker-agents) all enter Trial, and [Claude Skills](/ai-radar/techniques/#claude-skills) moves to Adopt. In [Languages & Frameworks](/ai-radar/languages-frameworks/), [AGENTS.md](/ai-radar/languages-frameworks/#agentsmd-and-steering-files) and the first-party [vendor agent SDKs](/ai-radar/languages-frameworks/#vendor-agent-sdks) enter Trial. [Spec-driven development](/ai-radar/techniques/#spec-driven-development) stays in Adopt.

A checker like Lean verifies that an agent's work is right, whereas guardrails limit what the agent can do. Some very public guardrail failures have occurred this quarter. A swarm of OpenAI's evaluation agents tasked with beating a benchmark escaped a test sandbox and reached Hugging Face's production systems seeking the benchmark's answers. The radar adds blips informed by this and other failures: [agent code sandboxes](/ai-radar/tools/#agent-code-sandboxes), [MCP gateways](/ai-radar/tools/#mcp-gateways), [AI supply-chain security](/ai-radar/tools/#ai-supply-chain-security) and [agentic vulnerability discovery](/ai-radar/tools/#agentic-vulnerability-discovery) enter [Tools](/ai-radar/tools/), and [agent identity](/ai-radar/techniques/#agent-identity) enters Techniques.

Even as the frontier labs continue to impress upon the world, and upon potential investors, how risky their models are, regulation is being deliberately kept loose. The EU's Digital Omnibus pushed the AI Act's high-risk obligations to December 2027 to help keep European AI competitive. It kept the duty to mark AI-generated content. [AI content provenance](/ai-radar/techniques/#ai-content-provenance) enters Techniques and [AI governance platforms](/ai-radar/platforms/#ai-governance-platforms) moves up to Trial.

For now, the only dependable constraints are the ones teams build for themselves.

_Henry Garner (CTO, JUXT), October 2026_

## Radar Overview

Our radar is organised into four main categories, each containing technologies evaluated across four adoption levels:

- **Adopt**: Technologies we recommend using now
- **Trial**: Worth exploring for new projects
- **Assess**: Keep under observation
- **Hold**: Not recommended for new projects
