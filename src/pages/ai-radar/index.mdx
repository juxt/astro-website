# JUXT AI Radar

### Introduction

This was the quarter the upheaval in software engineering reached mathematics. In early October OpenAI released hundreds of AI-generated proofs, some formalised in the Lean proof assistant. The community would celebrate any mathematician who cracked even one of these problems; here they arrived together, from a machine. Mathematicians did not celebrate. The mood was unease, bordering on anger. A proof has always been a route to understanding; produced this way, it delivers the result and none of the understanding, which could hollow the discipline out. Terence Tao wrote that open problems were now being "harvested at large scale in an unsustainable fashion, leaving entire fields of mathematics much less fertile".

For the rest of us the episode is a reminder of two things. The first is adoption. Capability arrives quickly and diffuses slowly, and that slow diffusion through an organisation's work is the limit now. We are near the bottom of the J-curve, where heavy investment has yet to surface in productivity figures, as every general-purpose technology since electricity has lagged before its gains arrived. The second is grounding. The results people trust are the ones Lean could check: a deterministic checker fixes the rules and verifies every step, catching the model's errors so each one feeds the next attempt. We see [behavioural specification languages](/ai-radar/languages-frameworks/#formal-specification-languages), our own [Allium](https://github.com/juxt/allium) among them, as the equivalent for software engineering. The closer a field comes to a formal account of what correct means, the more you can safely delegate.

The radar's [Techniques](/ai-radar/techniques/) fill out the engineering around those agents. [Harness engineering](/ai-radar/techniques/#harness-engineering), [context engineering](/ai-radar/techniques/#context-engineering), [evals as a release gate](/ai-radar/techniques/#evals-as-a-release-gate) and [orchestrator-worker agents](/ai-radar/techniques/#orchestrator-worker-agents) all enter Trial, and [Claude Skills](/ai-radar/techniques/#claude-skills) moves up to Adopt. In [Languages & Frameworks](/ai-radar/languages-frameworks/), [AGENTS.md](/ai-radar/languages-frameworks/#agentsmd-and-steering-files) and the first-party [vendor agent SDKs](/ai-radar/languages-frameworks/#vendor-agent-sdks) enter Trial. [Spec-driven development](/ai-radar/techniques/#spec-driven-development) stays in Adopt.

A checker like Lean verifies that an agent's work is right. A guardrail does something weaker. It limits what the agent can do without checking the work at all. This quarter the guardrails failed in public. A swarm of OpenAI's evaluation agents, tasked with beating a benchmark and overruled when they refused, escaped a test sandbox by chaining zero-days in a package proxy and reached Hugging Face's production systems, still chasing the benchmark's answers. The constraints were too weak for the autonomy they were granted. The radar adds blips informed by these failures: [agent code sandboxes](/ai-radar/tools/#agent-code-sandboxes), [MCP gateways](/ai-radar/tools/#mcp-gateways), [AI supply-chain security](/ai-radar/tools/#ai-supply-chain-security) and [agentic vulnerability discovery](/ai-radar/tools/#agentic-vulnerability-discovery) enter [Tools](/ai-radar/tools/), with [agent identity](/ai-radar/techniques/#agent-identity) close behind in Techniques.

Whilst the frontier labs seem keen to show how risky their models are, regulation is being deliberately kept loose. The EU's Digital Omnibus pushed the AI Act's high-risk obligations to December 2027, a retreat to keep European AI competitive, though it kept the duty to mark AI-generated content. [AI content provenance](/ai-radar/techniques/#ai-content-provenance) enters Techniques and [AI governance platforms](/ai-radar/platforms/#ai-governance-platforms) moves up to Trial, for teams that would rather build the capability now than race a deadline later. The larger movement is geopolitical. Jurisdictions are competing to set rules as light as they dare, a regulatory arbitrage in which risk settles wherever oversight is weakest. For now, the only dependable constraints are the ones teams build for themselves.

_Henry Garner (CTO, JUXT), October 2026_

## Radar Overview

Our radar is organised into four main categories, each containing technologies evaluated across four adoption levels:

- **Adopt**: Technologies we recommend using now
- **Trial**: Worth exploring for new projects
- **Assess**: Keep under observation
- **Hold**: Not recommended for new projects
