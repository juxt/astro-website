Introduction

This quarter, the upheaval AI has wrought on software engineering over the past couple of years reached another discipline: mathematics. In early October, OpenAI released hundreds of AI-generated proofs, many formalised using the Lean proof assistant. The community would likely have celebrated any mathematician who cracked even just one of these problems; yet here they arrived all together, solved mostly autonomously by one of OpenAI's unreleased models. Mathematicians did not celebrate. The mood was unease, bordering on anger.

A proof is more than its conclusion: much of its value is in the route to it, in the understanding built along the way. Eminent mathematician Terence Tao has likened the AI shortcut to "taking a helicopter to drop you off at the [summit]", where you reach the result but "miss all the benefits of the journey itself". Software has long allowed the same thing, code that runs even when no one quite understands how, but agents make it the default. They hand over a plausible result while the comprehension necessary to change or challenge it is skipped. Some call what's left "epistemic debt", or in its starker form "cognitive surrender".

One remedy is to invest time in specification. We see formal specification languages, our own Allium among them, as the way to mitigate epistemic debt in software development. A formal specification, unlike an informal one written in markdown, offers certain guarantees that the design hangs together, much as Lean does for a proof. It also points you to the parts of the code that need strong tests to validate what an agent produces. Writing one keeps you engaged with the essential complexity of the problem, and hands the machine only the incidental complexity of the implementation. The closer a field can state formally what "correct" means, the more it can safely delegate.

This quarter the radar includes several other techniques for guiding and constraining AI output. Harness engineering, context engineering, evals as a release gate and orchestrator-worker agents and AGENTS.md and steering files all enter Trial, and Claude Skills moves to Adopt. In Languages & Frameworks, the first-party vendor agent SDKs enter Trial. Spec-driven development stays in Adopt.

Checkers like the Lean proof assistant, or automated tests, verify that an agent's output is coherent, whereas guardrails limit what the agent is permitted to do. Some very public guardrail failures have occurred recently. A swarm of OpenAI's evaluation agents tasked with beating a benchmark escaped a test sandbox and reached Hugging Face's production systems seeking the benchmark's answers. The radar adds blips informed by this and other failures: agent code sandboxes, MCP gateways, AI supply-chain security and agentic vulnerability discovery enter Tools, and agent identity enters Techniques.

Even as the frontier labs keep impressing on the world—and on potential investors—how risky their models are, regulation is being deliberately kept loose. The EU's Digital Omnibus pushed the AI Act's high-risk obligations to December 2027 to help keep European AI competitive. It kept the duty to mark AI-generated content. AI content provenance enters Techniques and AI governance platforms moves up to Trial.

For now, the only dependable constraints are the ones teams build for themselves.

— Henry Garner (CTO, JUXT), October 2026

Radar overview

Our radar visualises the AI landscape across two dimensions: quadrants divide the space by category, while radiants indicate our confidence in each technology.

Quadrants

The radar is divided into four quadrants, each representing a category of AI technology:

Languages & frameworks

The frameworks, libraries, and protocols that underpin AI development. These are the software foundations your applications are built with.

Techniques

Methodologies and practices for building AI systems: approaches such as RAG, prompt engineering, agent design patterns and evaluation methodologies. The "how" of AI development.

Tools

Software that enhances AI development workflows without being embedded in your application code: IDE extensions, CLI utilities, testing frameworks and observability solutions.

Platforms

Infrastructure and managed services that host and run AI workloads: cloud AI services, vector databases, model serving platforms and MLOps infrastructure.

Radiants

Each item is placed on one of four concentric rings representing our recommended adoption stance:

Adopt

Technologies we have high confidence in. Proven in production with strong community support; we'd recommend them for appropriate use cases without hesitation.

Trial

Worth pursuing. These show strong promise and are mature enough to invest time in learning and piloting. Consider them for new projects where you can absorb some risk.

Assess

Worth exploring to understand how they might affect your organisation. Research is warranted, but they're not yet ready for serious investment.

Hold

Proceed with caution. Technologies that are immature, overhyped, or being superseded. Not recommended for new work, though existing usage may be fine.
